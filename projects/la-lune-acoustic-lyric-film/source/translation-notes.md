# French → English meaning decisions

The supplied lyrics match the official upload description. Apostrophes and line separation were normalized without adding text. The two verses and repeated title are preserved; recognition of unrelated subtitle-credit text was rejected as a model hallucination.

- **Son parfum noctambule**: “Her nocturnal scent.” The feminine pronoun refers to the personified Moon introduced by the verse. English places “nocturnal” before “scent”; each follows its own French word.
- **ne font qu’une**: “become just one.” The restrictive “just” follows the union of `ne` and `qu’une`, with no artificial continuous span across `font`. “Become” follows `font`; “one” follows `qu’une`.
- **elle surgit claire et nue**: “she appears bright and bare.” The personification is preserved without introducing a beloved or other character.
- **L’équipée sauvage**: “The wild expedition.” “The” and “expedition” share the source event `L’équipée`; “wild” follows `sauvage` despite the English word order.
- **Désorientée s’emmène**: “Disoriented, sets off.” “Sets off” is the complete English expression for the source verb; both English words share its timing.
- **En safari lunaire**: “On a lunar safari.” The necessary English article belongs to `safari`; the adjective remains separately linked to `lunaire`.
- **L’Odyssée soudaine**: “The sudden odyssey.” Article and noun share `L’Odyssée`, while “sudden” follows `soudaine`.
- **Escale balnéaire, atmosphère bleu Neptune**: “A seaside stopover, a Neptune-blue atmosphere.” Each article shares its noun's event. “Neptune-blue” uses the union of `bleu` and `Neptune`. The geographic/planetary image stays explicit.
- **Loin d’un souvenir solaire**: “Far from a sunlit memory.” “From a” follows `d’un`; “sunlit” and “memory” retain their separate reordered source links.

Every target token has source IDs. These are semantic highlight links, not timestamps of sung English. Equal size, weight and active/inactive contrast apply to both languages.
