# Preview-to-master visual parity review

The approved source uses a 1440×1080 4:3 moving picture and a 44.1 kHz stereo AAC soundtrack. Production samples its picture on a 60 fps timeline, draws lyric/edge layers at `n/60` seconds from the same timeline and measured band data as the browser, and copies the original AAC stream. The final source frame is held through the 59.503 ms audio tail.

The browser was checked at matching source times 45.0 and 62.1 seconds in both layouts against short complete-composition stills from the production path. Source picture, reading position, word color, edge response, portrait frame placement and portrait backdrop were compared. The first portrait diagnostic had an overly bright backdrop; the final adapter uses a blurred, multiplicatively dimmed source-derived fill to match the approved preview. The browser and native canvas rasterizers give different widths for the same macOS TTC font declaration; the adapter's measured 1.077 glyph-size calibration restores the Chrome word widths and centered reading geometry without altering lyric times.

All 25 source-clocked cues were then visited in the live browser in both formats. Their row breaks match the production scene exactly. The 4:3 layout has a two-row L07; all other cues are one row. The 9:16 layout has two-row L03, L04, L07 and L19; all other cues are one row. The split words match: L03 after “the,” L04 after “most,” L07 after “can’t,” L19 after “the.”

Source-clocked PNG references for frame indices `2700, 3678, 3726, 6186, 7776, 9210, 10461, 11400, 11592` are reproducible with `npm run render:parity-stills` and stored locally under `output/qa-reference/`. They sample the dense cue, first-refrain pause and entrance, held and repeated words, and source ending. These are local QA assets, not repository media. Passing this visual comparison does not substitute for the owner's actual-audio listening review; the separately recorded review and render authorization bind the current preview inputs.
