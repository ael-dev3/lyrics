# Editorial and visual lessons

The accepted treatment keeps the performance central: warm serif typography, equal Spanish and English emphasis, stable reading positions and a compact spectrum. Original camera cuts and musicians provide the motion. Portrait preserves the full source width above the lyrics; landscape uses continuous shading to support text over the footage.

## Choices to retain

- **Let each recording establish its own visual identity.** Match palette, motion and density to the current imagery and arrangement. A successful treatment from another track is a reference, not a template to repeat unchanged.
- **Use motion with a purpose.** Preserve meaningful source movement. Keep supporting graphics restrained when the performance already carries the energy; reserve stronger emphasis for genuinely stronger passages. Avoid unrelated shaking, blinking or decorative transitions.
- **Give both languages equal presence.** Maintain comparable size, weight, contrast and complete highlight coverage. Reflow a long translation instead of making it secondary. Keep word positions steady as color changes.
- **Protect the ending.** Spoken tags and held final words deserve the same precision as the chorus. Keep sufficient reading contrast while they remain visible, then clear added decoration so original credits stay legible.

## Defects to catch before delivery

| Problem | Why it matters | Reusable check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Only “love” lights while “you” stays neutral | The translation's complete meaning is split visually | Check small pronouns, auxiliaries and other grammatical completions, not only prominent nouns and verbs |
| English “I love you” lights together while Spanish “te” drops out | The two lanes present different visible spans | Inspect phrase completeness in both directions; match the smallest complete unit such as **te quiero ↔ I love you** |
| A broader group swallows an independently sung word | Fixing missing emphasis can introduce excessive emphasis | Keep explicit subjects, negation, degree and sustained words independent when they have their own events; preserve real gaps |
| Text is legible in a still but fades into a bright moving background | Source cuts and lights change local contrast | Review the actual footage at bright, busy and late-song moments in both formats |
| Audio and lyrics continue over a black or frozen picture | A healthy playback clock can conceal a broken visual layer | Confirm visibly advancing footage after load, seek and layout changes; keep a recovery control available |
| A lyrics-only diagnostic is presented as a complete preview | It hides the composition that will be judged in the finished film | Keep the main preview complete and label any isolated diagnostic explicitly |
| A check reproduces an incorrect map perfectly | Renderer consistency is not proof of correct meaning or acoustic timing | Review the intended correspondence separately, then verify the encoded result against the corrected map |

## Delivery and documentation

Preserve earlier deliveries when issuing a correction. Provide clearly separated landscape and portrait upload files, readable platform-specific covers, concise credited posting copy and checksums. Use a representative frame decoded from the current finished video for its gallery entry.

Document concrete choices, observed defects and evidence limits in neutral production language. Keep personal quotations, ratings, local account details and private review conversations out of public notes. A brief account of what improved is more useful than publishing a transcript of the working process.
