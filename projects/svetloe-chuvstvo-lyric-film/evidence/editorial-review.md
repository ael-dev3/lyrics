## Fine correspondence and complete meaning

The mapping preserves explicit subjects, possessives, conjunctions, negation and repetitions. The two `самое` words remain separate source events and independently activate the two **very** words. The superlative article shares the first event; **brightest** and **feeling** follow the adjective and noun. Hyphenated presentation cannot collapse the repeated acoustic words. The two `огромной` words likewise remain independent; the second adjective never inherits the first occurrence's boundary.

The comparison hook uses `Ты / как / искусство` → **You / are like / art**. The light hook uses `Ты / яркий / свет` → **You / are bright / a light**. Required English grammar receives the complete meaning focus rather than becoming permanently neutral. This allows the article to activate with the noun even when English places it before the adjective; its position stays fixed.

Other complete expansions include `рос` → **grew up**, `вспомню` → **will remember**, `стал` → **have become**, `полон` → **am full**, and genitive `сил` → **of strength**. Where the Russian explicit `я` appears, English **I** keeps that separate source event. Do not put the explicit subject into a predicate group for easier forward highlighting. In the ash line, **ash**, **not** and **become** are individually mapped to `пеплом`, `не` and `стать`; reordered English requires no invented target timings.

The noun list preserves **the river**, **the greenhouse**, **your laughter**, and **the wall** as separate images. Contextual noun articles share their own source nouns. `твой` → **your** remains separate from `смех` → **laughter**. `рядом / со / мной` has the fine English correspondence **next / to / me**; there is no need to group those three source words.

## Interpretive choices and limits

- **Светлое** combines the luminous image with a positive, uplifting feeling. **Brightest** retains that metaphor; it does not certify a philosophical or author-provided meaning. The deliberate “very, very” repetition follows the supplied refrain wording.
- **Не те** does not identify the expected people. **Those next to me won’t be the same** makes the negative identity contrast explicit. **Те** supplies both the English subject **those** and the complement **the same** after grammatical recasting. The contraction **won’t** carries `будут / не` together; **be** follows `будут`. Named relations and the stronger judgment in “wrong people” are not added. A compressed **others** draft was replaced because it obscured this contrast.
- **Дом** becomes **the home**, supported by the recalled domestic details. It does not become “my home”; the possessive is not spoken. Literal **the house** remains a reasonable alternative.
- **Парник** can describe a greenhouse, hotbed or covered growing frame. **Greenhouse** is a broad ordinary English reading; the lyric does not establish the structure's architecture.
- **Крепкий покой** becomes **deep peace**, an idiomatic English adjective/noun pair. The translation does not add duration or a guarantee of permanent calm.
- **Что знакомо до слёз** uses the concise adjective/result clause **so familiar it brings tears**. **It** refers to the same recalled things expressed by `что`; **familiar** follows `знакомо`; **so / brings** expresses the degree/result in `до`; **tears** stays individual at `слёз`. This grammatical reshaping retains the tears image and avoids a cumbersome literal phrase. It does not add a named addressee or a separate English vocal.
- **Самой заброшенной, ветхой избушке** becomes **the most derelict, crumbling cottage**. The superlative remains explicit. Crumbling describes dilapidation; it does not assert or animate a literal building collapse.
