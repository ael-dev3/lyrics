## Presentation correction

The previous midpoint rule reserved 10 ms on either side of a shared gap midpoint. That left 20 ms with neither line visible. Original-cadence frames landed in that empty interval at **57.800, 131.600, 179.680, 186.920 and 193.320 s**. In the final fly phrase, the line vanished only 10 ms after its selected lexical release. Several other short exit windows degenerated to immediate cuts or compressed fades.

The v4 layout has one mutually exclusive reading window per complete bilingual cue. It keeps the outgoing neutral line through a source-supported vocal-decay hold. The hold uses the upper edge of each reviewed tail range as a legibility choice, not a new phonetic word boundary. When sufficient space remains, a smooth 0.42–0.50 s fade follows that full-opacity hold. Otherwise the old line remains fully readable until the next actual vocal entrance and is replaced atomically, with the incoming line fully visible at its first word. There is no midpoint blackout or overlapping ghost text.

At v4, all 139 lexical sample events and meaning mappings remained unchanged from the reviewed v3 candidate. Word color released separately from the complete line. Source body versus reverberation remained uncertain in some passages; neutral line lifetime protected readability without pretending passive decay was new articulation. The current-cue control also followed the visible reading window, rather than declaring a cue absent while its line still belonged on screen.
